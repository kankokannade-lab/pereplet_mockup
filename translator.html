Captain Somov then stepped toward the railing and ordered the landing boats into the water. The sailors worked in complete silence, aware of the heavy surf pounding against the dark cliffs ahead. Within minutes the oars hit the freezing water, pulling away toward the silhouette of the old navigational tower. Nobody spoke of what had happened during the previous survey. The official report was locked away in the archives of the admiralty, yet every man in the boat knew the legends that surrounded this godforsaken fjord. The damp air carried the pungent odor of sea kelp and rotting pine timbers from the abandoned wharf. Lieutenant Morozov sprang onto the stones first, securing the hemp mooring line to an iron bollard. Above them rose the formidable granite outline of the old lighthouse, its darkened glass lantern reflecting the cold stars. Every footstep echoed hollowly against the wet stone walls of the canyon path leading upward to the iron gates. Somov raised his brass lamp, illuminating the worn inscription carved deep into the basalt lintel: 'Fortress of the North Wind, 1842'. The keyhole was rusted shut, yet the heavy bar had been lifted from inside.
